Fused Segmentation: Segmented trees and canopies from open pasture by combining the Excess Green Index (ExG, $2G - R - B$) with HSV green color-space thresholding. This makes segmentation highly robust to shadow variations and lighting shifts.
Ground Projection: Developed projection.py implementing coordinate mapping homography for UAV perspective projections based on camera height and gimbal pitch/roll parameters.
Objective 3: Hardened Baseline Tracker & Amodal Anchor Seeding
Baseline Tracker: Developed baseline_tracker.py implementing a multi-target bounding box tracker with a 2D/4D Kalman Filter and Hungarian association, augmented with rolling average HSV color histograms to serve as appearance embeddings.
Amodal Seeding: Developed amodal_anchor.py implementing virtual anchors. When a visible animal track centroid intersects with the projected occlusion mask, an AmodalAnchor is seeded, updating its position estimate via kinematic momentum blended with herd-cohesion velocity.
Hardened Lifecycle Policies:
Anchor Expiry: Virtual anchors automatically expire and clean themselves up after 150 frames in occlusion (approx. 30 seconds at 5 FPS).
Duplicate Suppression (Merge): If two active amodal anchors overlap with an IoU > 0.6 inside an occlusion zone, the newer anchor is suppressed in favor of the older anchor to prevent redundant tracks.
ReID Appearance Scoring: During re-emergence, candidate matches are validated using a combined score: 70% spatial IoU overlap and 30% appearance similarity (histogram intersection) to resolve ID switches.
Objective 4: Spatiotemporal Deformable Attention
Recurrent Query Updates: Developed attention.py implementing the spatiotemporal deformable attention module in PyTorch. For each active anchor, offsets and attention weights are predicted, features are retrieved using bilinear grid-sampling over a queue of past feature maps, and query embeddings are updated.
Objective 5: Trajectory Smoothing & Loss Functions
Hindsight Smoother: Developed loss.py containing the bi-directional Rauch-Tung-Striebel (RTS) smoother to interpolate true hidden paths during training.
Loss Functions: Implemented the Hindsight Temporal Loss ($\mathcal{L}{hind}$), Probabilistic Occupancy Loss ($\mathcal{L}{occ}$), and Bounded Hallucination Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{bound}$) to supervise network training.
Objective 6: Quantitative MOT Evaluation Module
MOT Metrics: Developed evaluation.py to calculate Multi-Object Tracking accuracy (MOTA), identity switches (IDSW), false positives, false negatives, and amodal-specific occlusion recovery success rates.

Counterfactual Amodal Anchors - Hardened Validation and Benchmark Report
This report presents the validation results, quantitative benchmark metrics, and system hardening enhancements implemented for the Beyond the Line of Sight: Counterfactual Amodal Anchors for Occlusion-Aware Multi-Animal Tracking in UAV-Based Precision Livestock Monitoring framework.
1. Hardened Tracking Stack Enhancements
To prepare the tracking stack for real-world scaling and resolve key operational risks, we implemented three critical robustness features in the tracking engine:
A. Anchor Expiry Policies
Problem: In extensive grazing pastures, animals may leave the camera field-of-view while occluded or remain hidden under dense vegetation indefinitely. Keeping anchors active indefinitely wastes memory and risks "hallucinating" trajectories that have departed.
Solution: We implemented an occlusion duration threshold of 150 frames (approx. 30 seconds at 5 FPS). If an anchor remains in the occlusion zone without a re-emergence trigger beyond this threshold, it is automatically expired and deleted from the active tracking space.
B. Duplicate Suppression & Anchor Merging
Problem: When multiple animals enter an occlusion zone in close proximity, standard motion projection can drift, causing predicted anchors to overlap and create redundant tracking tracks.
Solution: We added a duplicate suppression layer. If the predicted bounding boxes of two active amodal anchors overlap with an Intersection over Union (IoU) of > 0.6, the newer anchor (with shorter occlusion history) is suppressed in favor of the more established, older anchor.
C. HSV Histogram rolling Appearance Cues (ReID)
Problem: Bounding box IoU matching alone is highly sensitive to motion deviations, leading to identity switches (IDSW) when multiple animals emerge from the same canopy shadow simultaneously.
Solution: We integrated a lightweight color histogram appearance descriptor (HSV 8x8 Hue-Saturation bins) into the tracker.
When animals are visible, a rolling average of their HSV appearance histogram is updated ($\alpha=0.10$).
When a target enters occlusion, its appearance embedding is stored in the AmodalAnchor.
Upon re-emergence, candidate matches are scored using a blend of 70% spatial IoU overlap and 30% appearance similarity (histogram intersection), drastically reducing ID switches.
2. Robust Vegetation Segmentation
Classical segmentation indices like the Excess Green Index (ExG, $2G - R - B$) are highly susceptible to illumination shifts (cloud cover, direct sun highlights, dry grass reflections). To make the canopy shadow mapping robust, we implemented a Fused Masking Model:
ExG Masking: Extracts green foliage pixel indices based on relative channel ratios.
HSV Range Masking: Computes a binary mask matching green color space boundaries (Hue: 35–85, Saturation: 40–255, Value: 30–255).
Union Fusion: Applies a bitwise OR operation to combine both maps. This union captures shadowed canopy sections (which have high ExG but low saturation) as well as bright green clearings, which are then cleaned using morphological opening and closing filters to construct a stable ground-plane occlusion mask $\mathcal{M}_{occ}$.

Low-Performer Diagnostic & Tracker Upgrade Report
This document presents a diagnostic analysis of the low-performing video clips (DJI_0009, DJI_0010, DJI_0012) and details the technical blueprints to upgrade the tracking stack with CTRV Motion States, MobileNetV3 ReID Features, and Multi-Hypothesis Association.
1. Diagnostic Case Studies: Low-Performing Clips
During our dataset-wide evaluation, three clips underperformed compared to the rest of the dataset:

1.1. DJI_0012 Case Study: Long Occlusion Expiry
Observation: Occlusion recovery was 0.0%. Both amodal anchors expired after exceeding the 150-frame limit.
Failure Analysis: The animals entered a large, dense tree canopy and remained static or moved very slowly under cover for more than 30 seconds.
Mitigation: Implement an adaptive expiry threshold. Instead of a fixed 150 frames, check if the anchor's velocity is near-zero ($v < 0.2$ pixels/frame). If static, increase the expiry limit to 300 frames, as static animals under cover do not accumulate spatial motion uncertainty.
1.2. DJI_0009 Case Study: Camera Yaw & Homography Drift
Observation: High visible recall (100.0%) but low occluded recall (11.46%) and 9 failed recoveries.
Failure Analysis: The pilot executed rapid yaw maneuvers and altitude changes while tracking. This invalidated our planar homography assumption (static camera or slow linear movement) and caused the ground projected coordinates to drift away from the real physical locations.
Mitigation: Fuse the UAV IMU and gimbal telemetry directly into the camera extrinsics calculation, or employ optical flow camera motion compensation (CMC) using sparse ground features (ORB descriptors) to warp the state vector during frames with rapid yaw.
1.3. DJI_0010 Case Study: Dense Herd Clustering
Observation: Recovery rate of 16.67% with 8 failed recoveries.
Failure Analysis: A large group of cattle grazing together entered the same tree canopy block. The spatial overlap of their amodal anchors triggered the duplicate suppression threshold (IoU > 0.6), merging separate targets into a single anchor and causing multiple ID switches upon emergence.
Mitigation: Incorporate a group-aware cluster model. When multiple tracks enter the same occlusion zone, represent them as a single multi-hypothesis cluster anchor, keeping track of the total cow count, and delay individual track reassignment until they disperse.
2. Technical Blueprint: ReID & Motion Model Upgrades
To resolve these failure modes, we will implement three core upgrades:
2.1. Constant Turn Rate and Velocity (CTRV) Motion Model
Currently, our Kalman Filter uses a Constant Velocity (CV) model. For curved paths and yaw rotations, we upgrade the state vector to a 5-D CTRV state:
$$x = \begin{bmatrix} x & y & v & \theta & \omega \end{bmatrix}^T$$
where:
$v$ is the velocity magnitude.
$\theta$ is the heading angle.
$\omega$ is the yaw rate (turn rate).
The non-linear state transition function $f(x)$ is integrated using a Runge-Kutta 4th order (RK4) step, yielding significantly tighter search windows under curving trajectories.
2.2. Lightweight CNN ReID Embedding (MobileNetV3-Tiny)
To replace brittle HSV histograms under shadow/illumination changes, we integrate a tiny, pre-trained MobileNetV3 feature extractor running on GPU.

EMA Drift Guard: The appearance embedding is updated only if:
The target is fully visible (not near any occlusion boundary).
The appearance similarity between the current crop and the stored profile is above a gating threshold ($S_{sim} > 0.65$).
This prevents the embedding from drifting when an animal gets partially occluded or is subjected to sudden shadow transitions.
2.3. Multi-Hypothesis Tracking (MHT) for Crowded Emergence
For ambiguous re-emergences (multiple cows emerging from the same tree canopy), we instantiate a local tree of association hypotheses:
Hypothesis Branching: Generate a list of candidate associations based on spatial distance and ReID similarity.
Score Accumulation: Compute the cumulative path probability score over a temporal window of 5 frames.
Pruning: Prune branches with low path scores, keeping only the highest-scoring association. This resolves high-density association conflicts with high reliability.
